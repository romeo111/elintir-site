s.Link — telemetry, commands and video

Our own physical and data-link layers connecting a station to its operator. s.Link carries telemetry and commands; s.Link4k is a separate wideband profile for video.

Overview · Profiles · Trials · Modulations · Development

Overview

Two tasks, two radio profiles

s.Link · telemetry and commands
A direct radio channel between station and operator carrying bearings, equipment status and commands. The narrowband GMSK profile runs on hardware. Frequency hopping passed over-the-air trials in September.
s.Link4k · video
H.265 passes through fragmentation, packet error correction, encryption and the radio path. A video file recovered after transmission through antennas matched the original byte for byte. The profile name does not mean that 4K streaming has been demonstrated.
Data protection
ChaCha20-Poly1305 provides encryption and data authentication. Error correction helps recover damaged data and lost packets. These are separate mechanisms: encryption alone does not provide resistance to radio interference.
Operator interface
The console displays radio-link status, telemetry and video. s.Link supports an operator-silence mode.

Profiles

Specifications depend on the profile

Profile Physical layer Status
s.Link GMSK; 25 and 12.5 ksymbol/s profiles. Convolutional coding, Viterbi decoding and a frequency-hopping mode. Radio link and frequency hopping tested over the air, including 2 hops/s in trials on 9–12 September 2026. Half-rate profile tested in an RF loop.
s.Link4k QPSK, 15.36 Msymbol/s; 19.2 MHz bandwidth; 1,024-byte packet. Video file transmitted over the air and recovered using the host software reference decoder.

The baseline s.Link4k profile has a calculated rate of about 14.7 Mbit/s during a burst. Average video bitrate is lower because of gaps, overhead, loss correction and repetitions. Frequency, burst duration and transmit duty cycle depend on the selected configuration.

Modulations and applications

Choosing the waveform for the channel

Short telemetry messages, line-of-sight video and a ground robot among buildings require different channel properties. We compare waveforms for noise and reflection tolerance, throughput, latency and radio hardware requirements.

GMSK · telemetry and commands
The baseline s.Link modulation for small messages: bearings, equipment status and commands. Its compact spectrum and nearly constant signal amplitude suit a narrowband channel and its power amplifier. We study the balance between rate, sensitivity and packet duration. The baseline radio link has been tested over the air; slower profiles have separate validation.
CSS · short messages in difficult channels
Chirp spread spectrum is a candidate for telemetry when delivering a small message matters more than carrying a large stream. We study sensitivity and behaviour under different types of interference. The trade-off is between data rate, occupied bandwidth and transmission time. Our own modulator and receiver are being evaluated in software models as a separate research direction.
QPSK · line-of-sight video
Single-carrier QPSK is the baseline s.Link4k option for air-to-ground channels and elevated antennas with a clear line of sight. It combines throughput with moderate amplifier linearity requirements. This is the profile that has already carried a video file through the over-the-air path. Channel equalisation is being studied separately for more demanding reflections.
BPSK and repeated QPSK · lower-rate options
We study these as options for an adaptive video link when signal quality deteriorates. BPSK carries fewer bits per symbol; repeated QPSK spends more airtime on the same data. The aim is to reduce stream bitrate while maintaining connectivity. Both options are implemented in the software profile model; automatic switching in the complete radio path still needs validation.
16-QAM · more throughput in a good channel
A candidate for higher video bitrate when channel quality has sufficient margin. More bits per symbol increase throughput within the same bandwidth, but place greater demands on noise levels, reception accuracy and transmitter linearity. We study it as an upper adaptation step. It is implemented in the software model; its over-the-air readiness is assessed separately from baseline QPSK.
COFDM · video links for ground robots
A priority research option for unmanned ground vehicles: low antennas, buildings, trees and nearby vehicles create reflected signal copies that arrive with different delays. COFDM combines multiple subcarriers, a guard interval and error correction to better tolerate this multipath propagation. In our ground-channel simulations it outperformed the tested single-carrier receiver without a multi-tap equaliser. The trade-offs are overhead and higher amplifier and processing requirements. These are simulation results; COFDM trials on a real ground robot are still ahead.
CSS + DPSK / QAM · experimental hybrids
We are exploring whether a chirp signal can carry additional data through its phase or amplitude. Initial software probes have been performed for CSS with differential phase modulation using DBPSK and DQPSK. Combining CSS with QAM remains a research idea. We are investigating whether the benefit survives a real channel with imperfect synchronisation; these hybrids are not yet finished s.Link modes.

COFDM is a coded multicarrier scheme; our current model uses QPSK on its subcarriers. Frequency hopping changes the operating frequency and is described as a link mode. Coding, repetition and modulation selection are studied together while keeping their roles distinct.

Development

What we are developing next

FPGA reception and transmission
The s.Link4k receiver with a channel equaliser has been synthesised and routed for Zynq-7010. This is a separate milestone from over-the-air reception by a complete FPGA system. The next step is end-to-end hardware validation.
Real-time video
Integrating the camera, encoder, radio modem and console into a continuous path. Camera-to-display latency and sustained operation with packet loss still need to be measured.
Mesh and relaying
The s.Link4k transport packet format, encryption and outer loss correction are implemented. A controlled multi-node radio mesh with a scheduler requires further implementation and testing.
Antennas, amplification and range
Measuring output power, antenna performance and transmitter–receiver interaction. Range will be established through field trials with documented conditions, antenna heights and channel profiles.
